George H. LaBarre Galleries, Inc.

A charming story, a lifetime in collecting, and the largest inventory of collectible stocks and bonds in the world.

George LaBarreFounder

So many people have a charming story of how they were introduced to collecting. I suppose my start was typical. In 1955, at the age of nine, I was more than a bit excited to find stamp-approval ads at the back of comic books and in the classifieds of Boys’ Life magazine. These led to an album and stamp packets at Woolworth’s.

At eleven, I found myself looking over the stamp display at that favorite “5 & 10.” An older gentleman — about eighty — approached and asked if I liked stamps. My answer, a simple “Oh yes.” He explained that he had thousands of stamps on old post cards and encouraged me to have my dad bring me to visit on Saturday. The visit led to a few gifts of stamps, and before I left he asked if I’d like to see his coin collection. A foot-high stack of coin folders completely changed the direction of my life.

From that moment on, collecting and dealing in almost every topic imaginable enriched each day. My dealing started in 1957 — I would buy, sell, and trade coins with the other kids. At fourteen I made regular trips to Boston and knew many of the great dealers of the day.

Into business

The years flew by: a bit of college, four years in the Air Force including tours in Korea, Vietnam, and Washington, D.C., three years in banking, and finally, in 1973, a full-time business dealing in collectibles. Many of our Americana mail-auction catalogs were issued in the 1970s.

In 1980, we had the good fortune to encounter a large group of Boston, Hartford, and Erie Railroad bonds. These engraved bonds were beautiful — superb color and graphics, three great imprinted revenues, and rows of coupons all bearing a train vignette. This historic bond had every quality a collector could want. We sold the group quickly and, with that introduction to the hobby, we were hooked.

Whenever we had the opportunity to buy an interesting archive, we bought it. We are now proud to stock the largest inventory of collectible stocks and bonds in the world — over six million pieces. We handle certificates ranging from a dollar to tens of thousands (the record in this field is a Ford Motor Company of Canada stock signed by Henry Ford, selling at $150,000). A fine inventory of coins, paper money, autographs, presidential campaign items, and historically important Americana rounds out the offering.
